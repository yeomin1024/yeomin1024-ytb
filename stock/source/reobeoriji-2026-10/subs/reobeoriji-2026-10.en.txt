-도입
QLD and TQQQ follow the Nasdaq-100 at 2x and 3x.
Saved for years, they seem sure to leave far more, but how long can they last in a crash?
From 1999, let's compare 1x, 2x and 3x with a backtest.
-규칙
Like QLD and TQQQ, 2x and 3x are calculated: 2x or 3x the index each day, minus costs.
We buy $500 on the first trading day of each month, with a 0.25% fee.
-경주
Now let's roll the dates forward.
The blue line is QQQ, tracking the Nasdaq-100 at 1x; purple is 2x, orange is 3x.
Yellow dotted line: money put in so far.
Each step up the vertical scale is 10 times.
All three accounts reinvest the Nasdaq-100's dividends.
At the end of 1999, the 3x account was 3.28 times the $4,500 put in.
From March 2000, the dot-com bubble starts to burst.
On March 29, 2001, the 3x account had lost 92.3% of the $12,000 put in.
On April 4, 2x was down 80.7% and 1x 49.9%; all three were below the money put in.
Still, all three accounts kept buying $500 every month.
In 2003, 3x rose 167.4%.
At the end of 2007, all three were nearly equal.
-금융위기
Then came the 2008 financial crisis.
On November 20, the 3x account had lost 89.5% of the $58,000 put in.
On the same day, 2x had lost 72.8% and 1x 36.4%.
In 2009, 3x rose 196.4%.
Until the end of 2012, 1x was still the largest, but on July 3, 2013, 3x overtook 1x.
In June 2017, the 3x account passed $1,000,000.
In the COVID crash, 3x fell 69.7%, 2x 51.9% and 1x 28.5%.
Still, the 3x account had about $970,000, more than the $126,000 put in.
At the end of 2021, the 3x account had about $9,100,000, widening to 8.66 times 1x.
-긴 하락
In 2022 came a long decline.
The 3x account fell from about $9,300,000 on January 3 to about $1,800,000 on December 28.
That's a loss of 80.9%.
2x fell 62.8% and 1x 34.4%.
In 2023, 3x rose 198.2%.
In December 2024, the 3x account passed $10,000,000.
In the April 2025 plunge, 3x fell 56.8%, 2x 41.8% and 1x 22.7%.
-결과
October 15, 2026: the results.
All three accounts put in about $170,000 over 331 purchases.
The 1x account is about $2,000,000, and 2x about $7,900,000.
The 3x account was about $17,000,000.
The 3x account peaked on June 2, 2026, at about $20,000,000.
3x was 8.55 times 1x, and 2x was 3.91 times.
Since July 3, 2013, 3x has stayed above 1x.
In the 14.3 years before that, 3x was above 1x on only 10.8% of days.
Days above the money put in were also fewer for 3x: 1x 78.1%, 3x 64.7%.
-매일 맞추기
Why did 3x shrink so much in crashes?
Let's see what it means for a leveraged ETF to reset its multiple every day.
Say the index rises 10% one day and falls 10% the next.
1x loses 1%.
2x rises 20%, then falls 20%: a 4% loss.
3x rises 30%, then falls 30%: a 9% loss.
The more swings repeat and the bigger the multiple, the more is lost.
Even if the index comes back, leveraged accounts end up below where they started.
In fact, in 2018, 1x fell 0.1%, almost flat.
That year, 2x fell 8.6% and 3x 20.1%.
-한 번에
But what if you put it all in once at the start, not every month?
On April 1, 1999, each of the three accounts got $10,000.
On March 9, 2009, the 3x account had only $13 left: 99.87% was gone.
2x had $450.
1x hit its low of $3,736 on October 9, 2002.
Here are the values on October 15, 2026.
1x about $160,000, 2x about $130,000, 3x about $18,000.
With a lump sum, 3x kept only 11.4% of what 1x had.
The monthly accounts kept buying, even at prices cut by the crashes.
-시작 시점
April 1999 was right before the dot-com crash, so the start may have been bad.
What if it had started in another month?
We built 10-year monthly accounts, one starting each month from April 1999 to October 2016.
211 in total.
After 10 years, 3x beat 1x in 175 of the 211.
2x beat 1x in 183.
Accounts left below the money put in: 1x 4, 2x 16, 3x 20.
The worst was the account started in April 1999.
Of about $61,000 put in, 3x had about $12,000, 24.9% of 1x.
The best was the account started in September 2008, with 3x at 6.49 times 1x.
In all 31 of the 25-year accounts, 3x came out on top.
-실제 ETF
We should also check how closely the calculated 2x and 3x match real ETFs.
This is how the 2x and 3x accounts were calculated.
The two ETFs launched in 2006 and 2010, so from 1999 we have to calculate them.
Each day, they follow 2x or 3x the Nasdaq-100's daily return.
From that, we subtract a 0.95% fee and interest on borrowed money.
The interest is the US government's three-month rate plus 1.02%.
The 1.02% was set so the end value matches the real QLD from June 2006.
With the same value, TQQQ came out 0.57% lower than the real one on a 1-year basis.
Now let's look at the real ETFs.
From March 2010, the month after TQQQ launched, $500 every month.
The money put in is $100,000.
QQQ grew to about $620,000, and QLD to about $2,200,000.
TQQQ was about $5,300,000.
Over the same period, the calculated 3x had about $5,100,000, a bit less than the real one.
The real TQQQ account also fell 81.5% in 2022, and the QLD account 63.3%.
-정리
Let's sum up.
Buying monthly from 1999, 3x ended at 8.55 times 1x.
But in 2001, there was a time when 92.3% of the money put in was gone.
In 2008, it was down 89.5% too.
It took 14.3 years for 3x to stay ahead of 1x for good.
With one lump sum at the start, 3x kept only 11.4% of what 1x had.
Even in the 10-year accounts with other start months, 20 of the 211 left 3x below the money put in.
-한계
The Nasdaq-100 rose a lot over this period.
In the main test, the 2x and 3x values are calculated, not real ETFs.
If rates and fees change, the results change too.
And to choose 3x, you had to endure losing around 90% of the money put in, twice.
Taxes, currency and inflation are excluded.
It's not investment advice, but the result of checking rules with code.
