-도입
If it drops right after you buy, you doubt your timing.
Do 10 popular buy signals really raise your odds of success?
Let's backtest them from 2016 with SPY, an ETF that tracks the S&P 500.
Success means rising 5% above the buy price; falling 2% first is a failure.
Once decided, we sell the next day and buy again on the next signal.
We compare 2,000 accounts where only the dates are random.
-매달 첫 거래일
First, no signal: buy on the first trading day of each month.
To see if signals help, we first need a method with no signal.
Green trades succeeded, red ones failed.
Top right is the success rate so far; below it, the random accounts' average.
Random accounts use the same sell rule, with waiting days drawn from each method's record.
At the end of 2021, the success rate was 56.1%, above the random average of 51.7%.
But in 2022, 11 of 12 trades failed.
From 2023, it moved almost in step with the random average.
Result: 37 successes out of 80, 46.2%, slightly below the random average of 47.1%.
The gray band is the range covering 90% of random accounts: 41.5% to 53.2%.
Inside the band means no different from random, and the first-trading-day buy was inside.
-신고가 돌파
The second through fifth are trend signals that buy as prices rise.
They assume that once prices start rising, they keep rising.
Second: buy on a 20-day high breakout.
Buy when the close beats the highest close of the past 20 trading days.
At the end of 2019, its success rate was 58.8%, above the random average of 50.3%.
After selling in April 2022, there was no signal for almost three months.
That year, 5 of 6 trades failed.
After 20 decided trades, its success rate never fell below the random average.
Result: 31 successes out of 63, 49.2%.
Random average 47.1%; inside the 41.3% to 53.2% range, so we can't call it better.
-모멘텀
Third: even without a new high, buy if the price is up from a month ago.
Buy when today's close is higher than the close 21 trading days ago.
In June 2020, its success rate was 63.0%, well ahead of the random average of 50.9%.
But in 2022, 10 of 12 trades failed.
After selling in late February 2025, there was no signal for over two months.
Result: 42 successes out of 81, 51.9%.
It was near the top of the random range of 43.3% to 52.6%, but still inside.
-이동평균선
Fourth is the 20-day average line, often drawn on charts.
Buy on the day the close crosses above the 20-day average from below.
From July 2016 to January 2018, it succeeded five times in a row.
In December 2021, its success rate was 64.7%, well ahead of the random average of 51.8%.
Then, through July 2022, it failed six times in a row.
After 20 decided trades, it never fell below the random average.
Result: 37 successes out of 71, 52.1%, the highest of the four so far.
Still, it's inside the random range of 40.3% to 54.3%, so we can't call it better.
-골든 크로스
The fifth looks at two average lines together.
Buy when the short 5-day average crosses above the long 20-day average.
After selling in September 2017, the next buy came in February 2018.
In July 2022, its success rate was 40.0%, below the random average of 48.5%.
It had just failed six times in a row.
After that, it succeeded five times in a row through June 2023.
From 2024, 7 of 12 trades succeeded.
Result: 25 successes out of 50, exactly 50.0%.
Random average 47.4%; it was inside the 38.6% to 56.2% range.
It had the fewest trades of all 10, so the range was also wide.
-하루 하락
From the sixth on, the signals buy when prices fall.
The idea: buy cheaper, and it should rise more easily.
Sixth: if it falls 1% or more in a day, buy the next day.
In January 2019, its success rate was 35.0%, well behind the random average of 46.9%.
Because in 2018 alone, 8 of 12 trades failed.
In 2022 too, 13 of 20 failed.
After 20 decided trades, it was never above the random average, not even for a day.
Result: 44 successes out of 95, 46.3%.
The random average was 46.8% and the range 40.7% to 52.8%; it was inside.
-RSI
Seventh: buy when RSI is below 40.
RSI is a number comparing the size of gains and losses over the last 14 days.
Below 40, we treat it as having fallen a lot, and buy.
In November 2019, its success rate was 61.9%, well ahead of the random average of 49.3%.
But in the 2020 COVID crash, 5 of 7 trades failed.
After selling in November 2020, there was no signal for over 10 months.
And in 2022, 11 of 14 trades failed.
So it held stock on only 35% of days.
Result: 30 successes out of 62, 48.4%.
The random average was 46.7% and the range 38.0% to 56.0%; again, inside.
-VIX
Eighth: buy when the fear index, VIX, is 20 or higher.
A higher VIX means more people expect big market swings.
This tests the saying "buy when everyone is afraid."
After selling in November 2016, there was no signal for over 1 year.
In 2020, 11 of 22 trades succeeded, exactly half.
In 2022, 18 of 24 failed, including six in a row from August to October.
From 2023, 14 of 23 trades succeeded, back above half.
Result: 48 successes out of 101, 47.5%.
Almost the same as the random average of 47.3%, and inside the 41.3% to 53.6% range.
-단기 저점
Ninth: buy when it falls 5% below the high of the last 20 trading days.
A dip buy aimed at short drops.
After selling in July 2016, the next signal came in February 2018.
In the 2020 COVID crash, 6 of 9 trades failed.
In 2022 too, 11 of 15 failed.
It held stock on 23% of days, the fewest of all 10.
Result: 29 successes out of 59, 49.2%.
The random average was 46.5% and the range 38.0% to 55.8%; it was inside.
-고점 대비 하락
Last, the tenth: buy when it falls 5% below the highest close so far.
A dip buy with a longer view than the ninth.
This one, too, had no signal for over 1½ years after selling in July 2016.
At the end of 2019, its success rate was 56.5%, above the random average of 49.8%.
Because all five trades that year succeeded.
In the 2020 COVID crash, 6 of 10 failed.
In 2022, 19 of 26 failed, the most of all 10.
From 2023, 14 of 23 succeeded, back above half.
Result: 45 successes out of 94, 47.9%.
The random average was 47.1% and the range 40.8% to 53.6%; this one was inside too.
-종합
Let's look at all 10 results at once.
Dots are each method's success rate; the gray band covers 90% of random accounts.
The highest was buying on a cross above the 20-day line, at 52.1%.
The lowest was buying on the first trading day of each month, at 46.2%.
Four of the top five were trend signals that buy when prices rise.
But all 10 were inside the random range, no different from buying at random.
Even for the top one, the 20-day line cross, 13% of random accounts had a success rate that high or higher.
-다른 조건
Was the 2% failure line too loose?
Make it stricter at 1%, and the success rate for buying on any day drops to 33.6%.
Then, only the 20-day line cross came out above the range.
At 1.5%, no method fell outside the range.
These 10 years may have been special, so we changed the period to 1994 through 2015.
In this period, the 20-day line cross was the lowest of all 10, at 33.3%.
In this period too, all 10 were inside the random range.
-정리
Let's sum up.
In this backtest, no buy signal had a clearly higher success rate than buying at random.
Trend signals led slightly over these 10 years.
But in the 22 years before, two of the four were below the random average.
And in years of steady decline like 2022, every signal had more failures.
What changed the success rate most wasn't the buy signal, but the failure line.
-한계
These results use only SPY, with data from 2016.
Success and failure were judged on closes only; we sold the next day and paid a 0.25% fee.
So a few trades were successes that lost money, or failures that made money.
We only checked success rates; the size of each gain or loss needs its own look.
Interest on cash while waiting for signals, taxes, currency costs and inflation aren't included.
This isn't investment advice; it's the result of checking rules with code.
