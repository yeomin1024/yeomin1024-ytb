The windmill was on the map.

Not as a destination —
just as a landmark.
A thing to pass on the way
to somewhere else.

They had passed dozens like it.
Would pass dozens more.

But she slowed down when she saw it.

Not because it was remarkable.
Not because it marked anything important.
Just because the blades were turning
and the sky behind it
was doing something with the clouds
that she hadn't seen in a while.

He didn't ask why she slowed.

That was one of the better things
about traveling with someone long enough —
they stopped asking
why you stopped.

The map could wait.
The destination wasn't going anywhere.

Welcome, traveler. Rest here a while.
