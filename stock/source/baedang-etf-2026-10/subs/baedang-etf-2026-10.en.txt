-도입
Dividends coming in feel reassuring, so many people collect dividend ETFs.
But even after the tax withheld on every payout, is more left than in an index ETF?
Let's backtest SCHD, a US dividend stock ETF, against SPY, an S&P 500 ETF.
-규칙
From 2011, we buy $1,000 on the first trading day of every month.
Dividends are reinvested after 15% tax, and the fee is 0.25%.
-경주
Now let's run the dates forward.
The purple line is the SCHD account; the blue line is SPY's.
Yellow dotted line: money put in so far.
Above: each account's dividends received and tax paid.
$1,000 buys as many shares as it can, fractions included.
Until 2017, the lead changed 43 times as the two traded places.
From January 2, 2018, SPY was ahead.
In 2019, SCHD paid 63.1% more dividends than SPY, but SPY's account was ahead.
In the COVID crash, both accounts fell together until March 23.
The SCHD account fell 32.8%, SPY's 33.4%: similar losses.
-하락장
In 2022, a long bear market arrived.
In April, SCHD pulled ahead again.
That year SCHD fell 14.6% and SPY 22.2%, so SCHD fell less.
On January 6, 2023, SCHD was ahead by as much as about $33,000.
But on April 26, 2023, SPY took the lead again.
In 2025 SCHD's dividends were still 2.6× SPY's, but the account gap widened.
-결과
October 15, 2026: the results.
Each account put in $180,000 in 180 deposits.
The SCHD account had about $480,000; the SPY account, about $590,000.
The SPY account had 1.22× as much: about $110,000 more.
After April 26, 2023, SCHD never got ahead again.
SCHD was ahead on only 20.1% of all trading days.
SCHD received about $87,000 in dividends, 2.1× SPY's.
So it also paid about $13,000 in tax, 2.1× SPY's.
Dividends over the past 1 year: SCHD about $15,000, 2.5× SPY's.
Every year from 2012, the SCHD account received more dividends.
-구조
It got more dividends, so why was less left?
Let's see where dividends come from.
On a dividend day, the price usually drops by the dividend.
Company cash goes to shareholders, so the company is worth that much less.
So receiving a dividend doesn't grow your total account value.
But the tax is taken the moment you receive it.
Let's look at a real account.
On December 10, 2025, 1 SCHD share paid a $0.278 dividend.
For the whole account, that was $3,743.
15% tax, $561, was withheld, leaving $3,181.
This money, minus the fee, was reinvested: 115.1 shares at $27.57.
The index ETF pays dividends too, just less.
2025 dividend yield: SCHD 3.8%, SPY 1.2%.
The more dividends you get, the more tax you pay, and sooner.
-해마다
Without monthly deposit timing, how did the funds themselves do?
We compared each year's 1-year return, dividends reinvested, without tax or fees.
In 4 of the 14 years from 2012 to 2025, SCHD returned more.
In 2022: SCHD down 3.2%, SPY down 18.2%.
In 2023: SCHD up 4.6%, SPY up 26.2%.
In 2024: SCHD up 11.7%, SPY up 24.9%.
In 2025: SCHD up 4.3%, SPY up 17.7%.
-다른 조건
Did the tax cause the gap?
15% is the rate withheld on US dividends for investors in many countries.
With 0% tax: SCHD about $510,000, SPY about $600,000.
Even with no tax, SPY had 1.19× as much.
At 30% withheld, the gap grew to 1.25×.
What if you sell everything at the end and pay 15% tax on the gains?
SCHD about $450,000, SPY about $530,000: the gap shrank to 1.19×.
That's because SCHD had already paid tax through its dividends.
And instead of monthly, $100,000 all at once at the start?
SCHD about $610,000, SPY about $790,000: SPY had 1.29× as much.
-시작 시점
The November 2011 start may have skewed the result.
What if it had started in another month?
We made 5-year monthly accounts, one starting each month from November 2011 to October 2021.
120 in all.
SCHD ended with more in only 25 of the 120.
SCHD's biggest lead: the account started January 2018, at 1.15×.
Conversely, in the account started November 2020, SPY had 1.34×.
In the 10-year accounts, SCHD was ahead in 12 of 60.
-더 긴 기간
SCHD launched in 2011, so it missed the 2008 financial crisis.
If dividend stocks are strong in downturns, a span with the crisis may differ.
So we also tested VYM, a US high-dividend ETF launched in 2006.
From December 2006, $1,000 a month, same rules.
In the crisis, VYM fell 29.2% and SPY 33.7%, so VYM fell less.
Until late October 2011, VYM was ahead of SPY, at about $67,000.
Money put in by October 15, 2026: $239,000.
VYM about $810,000, SPY about $1,100,000: SPY had 1.35× as much.
Among the 10-year accounts, VYM ended with more in only 5 of 119.
-정리
To sum up.
We saved monthly for 15 years, with 15% tax withheld.
The SPY account was 1.22× the SCHD account.
Even with no tax, it was 1.19×.
SCHD got 2.1× the dividends, and paid correspondingly more tax.
In bear markets like 2022, SCHD fell less.
Other start months gave some accounts where SCHD led, but just 25 of 120.
VYM, tested from 2006, also trailed SPY in 114 of 119 accounts held 10 years.
-한계
This was a period when US large-cap growth stocks soared.
In a period when dividend stocks were stronger, results could differ.
Taking dividends out for living costs wasn't part of this calculation.
Tax rates vary by country and account; health-insurance costs, currency and inflation aren't included.
And the SPY side had to endure years like 2022's 22.2% drop.
This isn't investment advice; it's rules checked with code.
