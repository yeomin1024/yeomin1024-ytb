A deeply cinematic collection of adagio works for solo cello and felt piano, inspired by the emotional architecture of late Romantic music and the harmonic language of Rachmaninoff. These compositions move through grief, memory, winter, consolation, resignation, and quiet healing — not as dramatic narratives, but as intimate emotional spaces slowly unfolding in time.

At the center of the collection is the dialogue between two voices:

The felt piano — muted, warm, and nostalgic, closer to memory than performance. Its softened hammers and intimate resonance create the sensation of hearing music through fog, candlelight, rain, or distance.

The solo cello — deeply human, confessional, restrained. Never virtuosic for its own sake. Every phrase breathes with vulnerability, weight, and emotional honesty.

The harmonic world draws heavily from Rachmaninoff’s late-romantic DNA:
long melodic arcs, suspended resolutions, chromatic inner voices, minor keys filled with warmth rather than cold, and emotional peaks that arrive quietly rather than dramatically.

Each piece inhabits a different emotional room within the same cinematic universe:
midnight rain against windows, winter silence, fading photographs, autumn resignation, sacred consolation, remembered dances, and the calm that follows grief.

The production aesthetic blends:

cinematic intimacy
analog warmth
high-fidelity close recording
salon and cathedral acoustics
subtle mechanical piano texture
slow atmospheric pacing

This is music for:
late nights, reflection, solitude, reading, grief, healing, writing, rain, contemplation, and emotional stillness.

Not background music.
A space to remain with what is human.
