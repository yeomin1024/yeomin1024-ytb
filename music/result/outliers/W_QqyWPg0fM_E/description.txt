I felt compelled to make this. I just felt it and as soon as got the two tracks in my session everything seemed to align. Right Where It Belongs and Time (from Inception) share more than a musical similarity. They both ask the same unsettling question: what if the reality we trust isn't quite what it seems? What if all the world you think you know is an elaborate dream?

To get personal for a second..as I've approached the age of 40, I've found myself thinking more about time. Not just how quickly it passes, but how memory reshapes it and life seems to speed up. Milestones arrive before you're ready for them and every now and then you're left wondering how the years slipped by so quietly.

On the surface, the connection is obvious. Inception is built around dreams, while Trent's lyrics question whether the world we experience is real at all. But the deeper connection, for me, is that they both ask what happens when certainty begins to unravel. What happens when memory, time and experience blur together until you're no longer sure whether you're remembering life as it was... or simply the story you've told yourself about it.

The older I get, the more I find myself asking these questions. We all reach moments where we look back and wonder how we became the person we are today. Did we choose this path or did it slowly choose us? Do we have any free will at all?  

I have avoided using the word mashup in this work. It feels to trivial and flippant in something I feel a lot of emotion about. Ultimately,  bringing these two tracks together is my attempt to help us be at peace with that strange feeling that time moves faster every year and we didn't; have much of it in the first place anyway.  We really should live this one life to the full - enjoy, love and feel to the maximum. 

What can you hear and feel in these two songs existing together?

In terms of the music I used both the studio mix of Right Where It Belongs and the Alt V2 mix. The vocals are heavily affected on the original until the end as the mix opens up. I also felt the slightly sparser guitar figure sat better in this context. I did end up bringing in some of the original pianos from the album version - so both versions are in full effect here. 

As always, I like to re-iterate that I do not use any generative AI to make my music. The artefacts you hear in the vocals are a by product of stem splitting and pitch shifting. It's not ideal and I don't like how it affects the vocal but nothing can be done to repair or prevent it during this process.
