If you have $100,000 invested today, could you retire in 10 years? In this video I put real numbers behind that question by comparing $100,000 in the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD) with $100,000 in the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO) over the last 10 years: the dividends each one paid, what the shares were worth at the end, and which fund left you with more money. The answer surprises a lot of dividend investors: SCHD paid about $25,000 more in cash, but VOO built the bigger pile.

Then I get to the question that actually matters: how much do you need to retire? I explain the 4% rule in plain words (William Bengen's 1994 research), why a 4% withdrawal is not the same as a 4% dividend yield, what 3% inflation does to a $4,000-a-month lifestyle over 10 and 20 years, what sequence of returns risk means, and how to work backwards from your monthly expenses to a realistic portfolio target. Nothing here is a projection; it's what happened over one decade, used to show how the math works.
