The stock market is hitting all-time highs while millions of Americans feel poorer every month. That contradiction makes more sense once you realize the stock market and the real economy are no longer measuring the same thing.

This video explores why enormous amounts of global capital are flooding into U.S. stocks even as households struggle with higher prices, expensive housing, and declining purchasing power. When investors lose confidence in other markets, America becomes a financial safe haven, and much of that money flows into a handful of companies like Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Tesla, and Alphabet. At the same time, massive government debt and rising interest costs are putting increasing pressure on the Federal Reserve and Treasury. Policies designed to keep liquidity flowing and borrowing costs manageable can push asset prices higher while slowly weakening the value of the dollar.

That creates the illusion of prosperity. Stocks rise, portfolios grow, headlines celebrate record highs. But if your money buys less every year, being richer on paper doesn’t necessarily mean you’re actually getting richer.
