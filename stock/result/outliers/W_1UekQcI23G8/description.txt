The Nasdaq 100 fell 2.9%, its biggest daily drop since June, as the AI trade lost the high price investors had been paying. Tesla is cutting capex, the cost of insuring Meta's debt hit a record and Lam Research's guidance underwhelmed. Goldman Sachs called it "a trying couple of weeks for the AI complex". Goldman's prime brokerage data showed the largest 2-week net selling of global tech stocks in more than 10 years, with short leverage at the 99th percentile on a 5-year view, which also raises the risk of a short squeeze. Yet the equal-weight S&P 500 closed higher and the Dow was flat, as money rotated into consumer staples, utilities, healthcare and financials. Underneath, Goldman says lower-rated credit is "the eye of the storm": US dollar project finance borrowing has reached about $105 billion this year, almost all of it for data centres and chips, and Bernstein sees a $76 billion funding hole at CoreWeave. With the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.00%, the link between Goldman's AI leaders basket and the S&P 500 has jumped from 0.73 to 0.91.
