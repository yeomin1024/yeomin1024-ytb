-도입
This year I invested in chip stocks and lost 250,000,000 won, 70% of everything I owned.
I borrowed too, and the July crash force-sold my loan first.
I'm forty-four, my job is shaky, and my daughter's private tutoring academy fees come monthly.
I have 100,000,000 won left for retirement, and I haven't even told my wife.
I thought I should quit stocks.
But just as it isn't a loss unless you sell,
I figured it wasn't a real loss yet as long as I stayed in the stock market.
So to win back my principal, I decided to study properly, and I looked into 5 rules to follow.
-방법1
First, move from Korean stocks to US stocks.
"Gukjang" means Korean stocks, "mijang" US stocks.
Many retail "ants" buy only Korean stocks.
But KOSPI, Korea's main stock index, is now over half Samsung Electronics and SK hynix by market value.
So buying KOSPI basically means going all-in on one industry: semiconductors.
And even there, I put money only in semiconductors.
So when chips wobble, the whole market wobbles with them.
On July 13, KOSPI fell 8.95% in one day, and trading even halted for 20 minutes.
Then in all of July, it fell more than 34%.
Holding only chips like me, I took the full hit.
Long term, same story.
KOSPI first passed 2,000 in 2007, but it took over 3 years to get back to 2,000.
After that, until 2016, it sat between 1,900 and the low 2,000s for over 6 years.
Hence "Bakseupi": the index stuck in a box.
Meanwhile, the S&P 500, bundling America's 500 top companies, rose 81%.
Korean stocks didn't always lose.
KOSPI passed 4,000 last fall, and this June it climbed to 9,000.
US stocks also fell over 20% in 2022, and there's exchange-rate risk too.
Still, to avoid piling into one country and one industry, I decided to move to US stocks.
And not into one stock, but the S&P 500, an index that spreads money across 500 companies.
-방법2
Second, don't leave the market just because you lost big.
When people lose big, many call stocks gambling, sell everything and leave.
Until last week, I only thought about deleting my stock app.
But the moment you leave, the loss is locked in forever.
So instead of leaving, I decided to just stop trading.
I keep the account and buy nothing new until I finish studying.
In the S&P 500, I looked at the four big drops since 2000.
I left out dividends and looked at price only, so the numbers run a bit low.
If I had put in 100,000,000 won at the October 2007 peak, it would have been about 49,000,000 won at the March 2009 bottom.
If I had left here, that would have been the end.
But if I had stayed, I'd have 100,000,000 won back 4 years later.
Held until now, it would be about 500,000,000 won.
The dot-com crash, the COVID crash and the 2022 bear market were all the same.
All four times, those who stayed got back to the peak.
It took as little as 5 months, as long as 4 years 3 months.
But this is about an index spread across 500 companies.
A single stock might never recover.
-방법3
Third, don't just follow stock news; study the macroeconomy.
Macro means big forces, like interest rates, that move the whole market.
Many investors only watch stock news and charts.
I only read good news about chip stocks.
But the July crash wasn't one company's problem.
Money crowded into chips and leveraged (amplified) products.
Analysts said worries about US rate hikes, AI profitability and geopolitical tension all hit at once.
When rates rise, deposits pay better, so money flowing into stocks tends to shrink.
And when the exchange rate spikes, foreign investors tend to sell Korean stocks and leave.
Market debt was a signal too.
Margin loans from brokerages hit 38.6 trillion won on June 24, the highest this year.
That was six days after KOSPI first passed 9,000.
The whole market was running on debt.
So before buying, I'll check indicators like the long-short interest rate gap and bonds.
I'm not trying to time my buying and selling with this.
I'm checking whether I'm overextending right now.
-방법4
Fourth, build your own rules from what you've studied, and don't be swayed by others.
Many buy stocks heard in paid stock-tip chat rooms, on YouTube and in group chats.
A "leading room" is a paid chat room that picks stocks.
I paid for one for 2 years, and this June I even borrowed after seeing profit screenshots in a group chat.
And I wasn't the only one.
In June and July, the top net buy of "seohak ants", Korean retail investors buying US stocks, was SOXL, a 3x leveraged semiconductor ETF.
That ETF fell 48.9% from late May to July 30.
The Korea Capital Market Institute blamed frequent trading and overconfidence for low returns.
And 62% of individual investors who started in 2020 lost money.
America's Morningstar also found fund investors earned 1.2 percentage points less every year because of their buy-and-sell timing.
So I wrote my rules in a notebook.
No borrowing,
No more than 10% of my account in one stock,
And before buying, write my reasons in three lines. That's my idea for now.
If I can't write those three lines for a stock someone recommends, I don't buy it.
With rules, profit screenshots from others sway me less.
If it doesn't fit my rules, I just pass.
-방법5
Fifth, make slow profits while cutting risk as much as possible.
Many people rush to make up losses and go back to leverage and all-in bets.
I feel that pull most.
So the day after payday, I only buy within a set amount.
I calculated with the S&P 500: 1,000,000 won a month for 10 years, what would have happened?
I looked at 202 start months, any month between 2000 and 2016.
Only 8 ended in a loss.
That means in 194 of 202, I ended up with more than I put in.
The average multiple was 1.6 times what I put in.
Even in the worst case, it was 0.95 times what I put in.
Of course, past results don't guarantee the future.
On the other hand, trying to catch up fast with 3x leverage would have meant a 76.7% drop in March 2020 alone.
Rush ahead, and you may never arrive.
I believe that if I keep building up slowly, I can get back the money I lost someday.
-마무리
Even after losing everything I owned in stocks, I believe it isn't a real loss if I don't give up.
From today, I'll pause trading and try to follow the methods I mentioned.
I hope your investing goes well too.
