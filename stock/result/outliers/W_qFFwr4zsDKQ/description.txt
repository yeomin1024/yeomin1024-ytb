In this video, I explore a hypothetical 15-year investing plan using SCHD, the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF. Starting with zero shares at age 50, buying one share every week, and reinvesting dividends could build roughly 1,000 shares under the assumptions in my model.

But owning 1,000 shares doesn’t tell you how much dividend income you’ll receive. Dividend growth makes a major difference.

I break down:
• How 780 purchased shares could become roughly 1,000 through dividend reinvestment.
• Three dividend growth scenarios producing approximately $122, $284, or $410 in monthly-equivalent income at 65.
• Why inflation changes what that future income can actually buy.
• How investing more through a Roth IRA changes the projections.
• What five additional years of investing could do.
• Why consistency matters when investing daily, weekly, or monthly.

SCHD pays dividends quarterly. The monthly figures shown are annual projected dividend income divided by 12, not monthly payments. All projections are hypothetical and depend on the assumptions explained in the video.

I also share how losing my savings, retirement accounts, and investments forced me to rebuild—and why starting late is personal for me.

The two levers you can control are how much you invest and how long you keep investing. Future dividend growth is uncertain.
