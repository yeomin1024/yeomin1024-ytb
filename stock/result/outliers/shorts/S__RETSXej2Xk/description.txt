Rekha Jhunjhunwala is one of India's biggest stock market investors. Her publicly disclosed portfolio has been worth tens of thousands of crores, which means even dividends from her shareholdings can amount to enormous sums. A 2024 report estimated that she earned around ₹224 crore in dividend income during the March quarter from her portfolio.

The big question is: how do rich investors earn crores without selling their shares?

The answer is dividends.

When a company earns profits, its board may decide to distribute part of those profits to shareholders as dividends. The more shares you own, the larger your potential dividend payment.

In India, investors often look at dividend-paying companies such as:

• IOC
• ONGC
• Hindustan Zinc
• Coal India
• Taparia Tools

Taparia Tools, for example, has historically declared substantial dividends, although dividends can change from year to year. Its FY2025–26 director's report showed an interim dividend of ₹32.50 per share and a proposed final dividend of ₹35 per share.

But remember: a high dividend does not automatically mean a stock is a good investment. Companies can reduce or stop dividends, and the share price can also fall.

The real lesson is simple: instead of only looking at stock prices, understand how shareholders can potentially earn through dividends, capital appreciation and long-term business growth.
